# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Trackora primarily serves logistics operators at shipping companies: delivery managers, dispatch teams, COD reconciliation staff, and operations leaders who need tighter control over last-mile work. Their context is practical and time-sensitive. They are dealing with live shipments, courier capacity, customer status calls, failed deliveries, cash collection, and fraud risk across Arabic-first workflows.

Secondary users include ecommerce merchants and social sellers who rely on the logistics operator for delivery visibility, COD handling, and clear customer tracking.

## Product Purpose

Trackora is a last-mile delivery management platform. The website explains how the platform replaces spreadsheet-heavy shipment handling with order upload, risk checks, smart dispatch, courier workflows, COD management, public tracking, and operational reporting.

Success for the website means a logistics operator can quickly understand what Trackora does, trust that it fits Arabic-speaking delivery operations, compare the product areas, and either request a demo or test public tracking without friction.

## Brand Personality

Confident, clear, operational.

The voice should feel practical and grounded in the work of delivery operations. It should avoid vague SaaS grandstanding and focus on specific nouns, visible workflows, and direct outcomes: shipments assigned, COD reconciled, risks flagged, customers informed.

## Anti-references

Do not let Trackora look like a generic SaaS template: repetitive icon-card grids, decorative gradient text, interchangeable dashboard mockups, vague buzzwords, tiny repeated section eyebrows, or muted text that reads poorly on tinted backgrounds.

Avoid making the brand feel like an abstract technology vendor disconnected from logistics work. The interface should not over-index on glossy decoration when the product story needs operational clarity.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with operational proof: show shipment, dispatch, courier, COD, risk, and tracking workflows as concrete product moments.
2. Respect Arabic-first reading: RTL layouts, Arabic typography, copy rhythm, and localized numbers should feel native rather than translated after the fact.
3. Make the next action obvious: demo requests, pricing exploration, feature navigation, and shipment tracking should be easy to find and easy to complete.
4. Use polish to build trust: visual craft should communicate reliability, speed, and control without hiding the practical work behind generic decoration.
5. Keep bilingual parity: English pages should preserve the same product meaning and interaction quality as Arabic pages.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Body text and placeholders must meet contrast requirements, focus states must be visible, interactive controls must be keyboard accessible, and motion must respect reduced-motion preferences. Bilingual RTL/LTR behavior is part of accessibility: direction, alignment, navigation, forms, and metadata should switch correctly by locale.
